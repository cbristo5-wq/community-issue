Understanding the Bottleneck

Why does traffic gridlock happen at UNCC, and why isn't fixing it as simple as reallocating money?

Simultaneous Arrival Curves

Standard 50 and 75-minute block schedules force thousands of drivers onto campus roads at exact identical intervals, creating artificial surge peaks.

Municipal Intersection Off-Flow

Internal campus roads dump traffic directly onto heavy Charlotte DOT arterials (W.T. Harris, N. Tryon, University City Blvd) without dedicated turn bypasses.

Blind Spot Searching

Without real-time deck occupancy indicators, drivers continuously circle decks like Union or Craver hoping for open spots, blocking incoming traffic lanes.

Preventing Off-Campus Squatting

How Smart Geofenced Queueing Works

A major concern with spot reservation queues is drivers reserving spots hours early from their bedroom or off-campus apartments. The NinerFlow queueing system fixes this with 3 anti-abuse controls:

1. 0.5-Mile Geofence Radius

App GPS unlocks queueing capability ONLY when your vehicle enters within 0.5 miles of campus boundaries (e.g., crossing W.T. Harris or University City Blvd).

2. Strict 7-Minute Check-in Timer

Once assigned a spot queue, you have 7 minutes to pass the deck gate LPR (License Plate Reader). Unoccupied spots automatically drop back into the live pool.

3. License Plate Recognition (LPR) Sync

Existing UNCC parking permits link directly to vehicle license plates. Gate cameras instantly verify registered queue holders upon arrival.

Policy & Budget Reality Check

Why Can't UNCC Cut Sports Funding for Traffic?

A common suggestion is: "Why not take millions from Athletics and build wider roads or free shuttles?" While it sounds intuitive, state policy and university accounting laws make this legally impossible.

1

Auxiliary Enterprise Status

Under North Carolina state policy, UNCC Athletics operates as an independent "auxiliary enterprise". It does not receive general state tax dollars or academic tuition.

2

Restricted Revenue Streams

Sports funding comes strictly from dedicated sports student fees, ticket sales, media rights, private donations, and NC sports wagering taxes. By law, these funds cannot be transferred to civil roads or general campus infrastructure.

3

The Real Solution

Campus civil improvements must be funded through state capital improvement funds, municipal partnerships with the City of Charlotte / NCDOT, parking auxiliary revenues, and federal transit grants.
